      Berlin, summer 1878

      Draw the borders

      The Great Powers have handed you the pen. Drag a finger across the land to draw a border. It splits the map when it runs from coast to coast, or joins another line.

      Claim the pieces

      Pick a country from the bar at the bottom, then tap a piece of land to give it to them. Drag across several to claim them all. Press and hold anywhere to see who lives there. Use two fingers to move and zoom.

      Sign the treaty

      Your borders are scored for wars (peoples stranded abroad, holy sites in foreign hands) and revolts (hostile peoples forced together). Each day brings a new puzzle with three tries to reach 🟩. Free play lets you redraw the whole peninsula.

      About the data

      The ethnic map is an original, simplified reconstruction made for this game. About 330 regional anchor points give a density and an ethnic mix; the map is interpolated between them, and cities are added on top. It is not a scholarly dataset.

      The mixes draw on censuses and period maps: the Austro-Hungarian census of Bosnia (1879), the Hungarian census (1880), the Eastern Rumelian census (1880), Romanian figures for Dobruja (1878–80), the Ottoman census of 1881/82–93, Ottoman figures for Thessaly (1877/8), and the ethnographic maps of Kiepert, Synvet and Stanford.

      Period sources disagree, above all about Macedonia, Thrace, Epirus and Kosovo. Each nationality's cartographers drew maps favouring their own claims. Where they conflict, this game takes a middle reading. "Macedonian Slavs" is a neutral label: in 1878 most were counted as Bulgarians, while Serbian and Greek writers claimed many of them. In the game they feel most at home in a Macedonian or Bulgarian state.
